Hunter Pets

What Forever changed, the best pet for each job and where to tame it, how to keep it strong, and what happens when one of you dies.

Your pick: Horde Skyborne Hunter

On Zephras Isle (level 10): after the Valanaar taming quests, tame an Ornery Galestrider (Tallstrider, level 8–9, Gustberry Lowlands). It swings every 1.3 sec, the fastest beast on the island, and Dust Cloud cuts armor. The Vuldren foxes look great but swing at 2.0 sec, so they're a looks pick.

On the mainland (12+): stable the strider and tame a Savannah Cub in the northern Barrens. It's a cat (+10% damage family), swings every 1.2 sec, and isn't a rare. It's level 4–5, so it catches up in a few kills. Keep it until your 20s, then trade up to a Starving Mountain Lion (1.2 sec, Hillsbrad) and later hunt Broken Tooth (1.0 sec, Badlands rare, level 37, 7–8 hour respawn) to keep to 60.

Pulling packs? Keep a bear in the stable. Swipe hits three targets and it eats anything.

In the beta, pets tamed on Zephras Isle sometimes lost their abilities after a dismiss or relog. Open the pet tab in your spellbook after logging in.

What Forever changed for pets

  • Pets scale from your gear. Your pet gets 10% of your attack power and all of your crit (melee or ranged, whichever is higher). Upgrading your gear upgrades the pet. Beta players also measured 2 pet health per point of your Stamina and 30% of your armor; Blizzard hasn't published those.
  • No player buffs on pets. A priest's Fortitude or a druid's Mark won't land on your cat any more.
  • Every family has exactly one unique ability, plus Bite or Claw (some get both) and Dive or Dash. Owls are now Bird of Prey.
  • Foxes are new. Trickster's Dance: +50% dodge and 30% faster attacks for 12 sec, 3 min cooldown.
  • Attack speed comes from the beast. All pets start at 2.0 sec. Each tame carries a hidden "Faster Attack" passive that restores its Classic speed (down to 1.0 sec). Since 2 November, untraining a pet no longer wipes that passive. Check the tooltip anyway after you untrain.
  • Focus regenerates smoothly, about 10 per second, instead of in ticks.
  • You can't tame beasts above your level (since 24 September).
  • Aggressive mode is back, in the pet tab of your spellbook.
  • Lone Wolf (Marksmanship, 11 points): +20% damage with no pet out. Not for leveling.
  • A durability fix is coming on 10 November. Blizzard said pets aren't tanking as well as they should and will be buffed to roughly Classic Era toughness. No numbers yet. Recheck tank-pet advice after it lands.

Best pet for the job

For solo play, damage usually wins: pick the fastest cat you can reach. Pick a tank pet only if you pull several mobs at once.

Leveling (damage)

Cat, then Raptor

Cats have the top family damage bonus (+10%) and most of the fast tames in the game. Raptors match the bonus and add a bleed, but none swing faster than 2.0.

Cat's Prowl needs Hunter level 30, so no stealth openers before then.

Leveling (tank)

Bear, Boar, Turtle

Bear: Swipe hits 3 targets, best for packs, eats anything. Boar: Charge roots the target and opens with threat. Turtle: Shell Shield halves damage taken for 12 sec, but needs pet level 20 (Kresh in Wailing Caverns is level 20).

Tank families do up to 10% less damage.

Dungeons and raids

Cat, or Wolf

Cat for raw damage. Wolf's Furious Howl adds melee attack power to the party within 15 yards (cut 40% in beta, still handy with no warrior). A 1.2 sec wolf is competitive on damage too.

Tallstrider's Dust Cloud lowers armor if nobody else does.

PvP

Broken Tooth, Wind Serpent

Broken Tooth's 1.0 sec swing pushes back caster spells constantly. Wind Serpent's Lightning Breath ignores armor and works at range.

In premades: Crocolisk Dismember (healing −25%, 6 sec, nerfed from 50%), Bird of Prey Mine! (3 sec disarm), Bat Sonic Blast (casts 30% slower).

Solo at 60

Wolf or Boar

Furious Howl buffs the wolf and you, which builds threat. A boar takes sustained hits and Charge keeps mobs off you.

Taming safely

  • The beast must be your level or lower. You need a free slot: stable your current pet first, and you can't tame while a dead pet is in your stable.
  • While you channel Tame Beast, your armor drops to zero. Open with Concussive Shot from max range, or drop a Freezing Trap and tame from beside it. Aspect of the Monkey helps you dodge.
  • If another player hits the beast, or it hits something else, the tame fails.
  • You get one active pet and two stable slots (buy them from any Stable Master). That's enough for a damage pet, a tank pet and a spare for learning skills.

Getting the most from your pet

  • Keep it Happy. Happy = 125% damage, Content = 100%, Unhappy = 75%. Feed it out of combat (you can't feed in combat). Mouse over the happiness icon to see what it eats. Carry a stack of its food.
  • Don't dismiss it to tidy up. Dismiss Pet costs 50 happiness. A pet dying costs a lot more.
  • Loyalty climbs from Rebellious → Unruly → Submissive → Dependable → Faithful → Best Friend as you feed it and kill with it. Higher loyalty gives more training points (300 at 60 and Best Friend). A pet tamed at a high level can start with negative points; just keep using it.
  • Pet XP: 25% of the XP you get from each kill (green mobs or higher). No rested or quest XP for pets. It stops at your level.
  • Growl comes from the Pet Trainer every 10 levels and works on every pet. Keep it on autocast while solo, and turn it off in groups so the pet doesn't steal threat from the tank.
  • Teach it skills: tame a beast that knows the skill (say, Bite rank 3), use it in combat until chat says you learned it, then train it to your main pet with Beast Training. Pets hold 4 active abilities. Pet Trainers sell Great Stamina, Natural Armor and resistances, and charge a small fee to untrain.
  • Mend Pet during fights, not after; it's cheap healing. Improved Mend Pet (Beast Mastery) also lowers its mana cost.
  • Beast Mastery picks: Endurance Training now includes Thick Hide (health and armor). Focused Fire raises your and your pet's damage. Summon Hawk (16 points) adds up to two hawks for 18 sec. Intimidation also doubles the crit chance of the pet's next hit.
  • Send in, then shoot: let the pet hit 2–3 times before your first big shot. With Disengage (threat reduction doubled in Forever) you can hand aggro back if something turns on you.

How death works

When your pet dies

  • It loses a big chunk of happiness, and you bring it back with Revive Pet (learned from Training the Beast at 10). It comes back low on health; Mend it or eat a break before the next pull.
  • In Classic, Revive Pet was a 10 sec cast that brought the pet back at 15% health (the Improved Revive Pet talent made it faster and stronger). Forever hasn't published its numbers. Check the tooltip.
  • A dead pet still takes its slot. You can't tame a new beast until it's revived.
  • Dying doesn't make you lose the pet. In Classic, a pet left Unhappy for a long time could lose loyalty and run away for good. Nobody has confirmed whether Forever keeps that, so keep it fed.

When you die

  • Your pet vanishes with you. After you come back, cast Call Pet; if it died before you did, use Revive Pet.
  • Click Release Spirit. You appear as a ghost at the nearest graveyard and run faster than normal.
  • Corpse run (use this almost every time): run back to your body and click Resurrect. It's free, apart from normal death durability damage. You come back with part of your health and mana.
  • Spirit Healer: come back on the spot at the graveyard, but your gear takes 25% durability damage (cut from 50% in beta) and you get Resurrection Sickness. Game files show −75% stats and damage for 1 minute per level, up to 10 minutes. Blizzard hasn't confirmed this. Only use it when your body is somewhere you can't safely reach.
  • A priest, paladin or shaman can resurrect you without any sickness. If one is in your group, wait instead of releasing.
  • Red items on your character sheet are broken and give no stats. Repair at any vendor with an anvil icon.

Is death permanent?

  • No, not on the launch rulesets (Normal, PvP, Roleplay). You always come back.
  • Hardcore (one life) is a separate ruleset coming after launch, rumored for winter. There, a death ends that character's Hardcore run and Blizzard says it can move to another ruleset. The full rules aren't out yet.
